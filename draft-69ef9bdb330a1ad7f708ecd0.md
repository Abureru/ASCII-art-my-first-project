Desmos is an online graphing calculator that enables users to create anything as long as they have the skills to manipulate functions, relations, expressions, and other mathematical techniques. In this section, we will focus on the interface and some useful features to customize and navigate Desmos.

To access Desmos, search for DESMOS GRAPHING CALCULATOR on any browser. They do have an app for those who are dedicated. It is highly recommended to create an account to ensure your work is saved.

For other download methods (e.g. Linux ubuntu) please check [here](https://github.com/DingShizhe/Desmos-Desktop).

## 1\. Interface

![](https://cdn.hashnode.com/uploads/covers/69ba1127c22d3eeb8a24feaf/0cab088e-63d2-495e-87ad-c7019e530f8a.png align="center")

This is the Desmos interface, there are five main things you need to know about. The cartesian plane (axis and grids), expression panel (on the left), keyboard (on the bottom left), open graph (file icon on the top left), and the Save button. But I will also briefly explain some additional icons.

### \----- Wrench -----

![](https://cdn.hashnode.com/uploads/covers/69ba1127c22d3eeb8a24feaf/f413bec6-8ddd-451a-a932-08fb63431640.png align="center")

The wrench icon contains options to modify your interface. The current image shows the default settings. Instead of including screenshots for every change, I will briefly explain each option so you can try it out yourself.

*   The "A" icon changes the size of text on the screen
    
*   Reverse contrast inverts the colour on the interface (great for night use)
    
*   Braille mode converts text on the screen into braille (idk why...not that I can feel the bumps on my screen...hopefully...)
    
*   The grid refers to the small boxes displayed on the graphing plane. You can keep the grid on or off using the checkbox. And if you want grids, you have the choice between orthogonal and polar.
    
*   Axis number display values along the x and y axis, and minor gridlines are the lighter grey lines between the main gridlines. Arrows adds arrowheads to the end of the axis.
    
*   The x and y-axis can be kept on or off. Labels allow you display custom text at the ends of the axes. You can also change its domain and range, although you can also adjust the view manually by dragging around the graph.
    
*   In the more options, you can change your axis to linear or logarithmic, and the lock viewport locks your plane so you can't drag or move around.
    
*   Complex mode for those who wants to test their imagination. You can also choose between radians and degrees.
    

Underneath the wrench the `+ -` icons allows you to zoom in and out on the plane. A house icon would appear underneath it when you do so, and you can click that to bring the plane back to its original state.

### \----- Keyboard -----

![](https://cdn.hashnode.com/uploads/covers/69ba1127c22d3eeb8a24feaf/c43ca45c-5ce8-4751-a396-284ac53e21b9.png align="center")

The keyboard icon opens the Desmos keyboard. The ABC section provides additional letters and some other symbols, sound button to hear the sound of the graph, and functions gives you useful built-in functions.

### \----- + icon -----

![](https://cdn.hashnode.com/uploads/covers/69ba1127c22d3eeb8a24feaf/1f9be8b2-c8b4-49ff-8154-275882cb9f9e.png align="center")

The plus icon allows you to insert and organise expressions within the left panel.

*   Expression gives you a new line to write your math. You can also press Enter on your keyboard or click on the next empty line.
    
*   Note allows your input to be a regular text rather than mathematical expressions. It is useful when you are working on something big and want to describe what an expression does, or just as a reminder for any modification you have to do in the future.
    
*   Table gives you a table in the expression. You can enter values and dots will appear in the plane (there might be additional uses for tables that are beyond this guide).
    
*   Inference allows you to create and manipulate probability distributions, quantitative data, and categorical data.
    
*   Folder allows you to organise expressions into groups. You can drag expressions in or create new expressions by hitting enter in the folder or just drag more in. You can also name your folder
    
*   Image allows you to insert images into the plane. This is especially useful for trace over artworks or references (which is what I mainly use Desmos for).
    

![](https://cdn.hashnode.com/uploads/covers/69ba1127c22d3eeb8a24feaf/e037cb92-543a-4ce4-bfaf-0d3705be44c4.png align="center")

For the sake of demonstration, I will use very simple equations.

When you have an equation, you can make it disappear by clicking the circle icon next to it. If a warning triangle appears, it means there is an error in the expression, and you can hover or click on it to see Desmos hints or error messages. You can also expand the expression panel by dragging the shadow line where it meets with the plane.

### \-----Setting-----

![](https://cdn.hashnode.com/uploads/covers/69ba1127c22d3eeb8a24feaf/0242ff0e-b7e7-404f-b66d-e71e12b06f3a.png align="center")

The setting icon allows customizing the appearances of expressions. When it comes to the appearance of your equations. To modify an expression, click the settings button first then click the coloured circle beside the expression.

*   The lines slider let you decide if you want to keep the outline of the expression.
    
*   The overlapping squares icon controls the opacity of the expression.
    
*   The line style options allow you to choose between solid and dotted lines.
    
*   The stacked line icon controls the thickness of the line.
    
*   The colour palette allows you to change the colour of the expression. You can also import more colours but more about that later.
    
*   The table icon again plots dots on that line based on your input into the cells of the table.
    
*   The two-paper icon is the copy button which duplicates the expression directly below. You can also just highlight the expression and control c control v it.
    
*   The cross button can be used to delete the expression. You can also delete it by clicking Backspace or Delete key enough times.
    

### \----- File -----

![](https://cdn.hashnode.com/uploads/covers/69ba1127c22d3eeb8a24feaf/e452bf1b-0f1e-4a23-9b9b-a0c9d4f04f71.png align="center")

The file on the top left corner allows you to see your past works. It records the last time the graph was opened or edited.

*   The New Graph button lets you create a new graph, geometry, 3D graph, or a new folder to store multiple graphs in one place.
    
*   The Saved Graphs shows all the graphs you have worked on and SAVED.
    
*   The Examples section shows you all the graphing examples provided by Desmos that includes example graphs and explanations (sometimes).
    
*   The Search by title lets you search up a specific graph you have done based on what you named it (not very useful in my case).
    
*   The Three lined reversed triangle icon filters the type of graphs that appear in your Saved Graphs (at the moment there are only 3D, graphing, and geometry).
    
*   The square and dot point line icons switch between viewing layouts for Saved Graphs.

## 2\. Basic Navigation Shortcuts

Desmos also supports several shortcuts and controls that can make graphing faster and easier.

### Mouse Controls

*   **Scroll Wheel** — Zoom in and out of the graph
    
*   **Left Click + Drag** — Move around the graphing plane
    
*   **Click Expressions on Plane** — Find and can quickly edit an expression
    

### Keyboard Shortcuts

*   **Enter** — Create a new expression line
    
*   **Ctrl + Z** — Undo
    
*   **Ctrl + Y** — Redo
    
*   **Ctrl + A** — Select all in an expression
    
*   **Ctrl + C** — Copy selected expression
    
*   **Ctrl + V** — Paste copied expression
    
*   **Ctrl + X** — Delete selected expression
    
*   **Delete / Backspace** — Delete expressions or characters
    
*   **Tab** — Move between table cells or editable areas
    
*   **Arrow Keys** — Navigate through expressions
    

### Graph Controls (in Desmos)

*   **Home Icon** — Reset the graph back to the default view
    
*   **Click and Drag the Expression Panel Border** — Resize the expression panel
    

**Click the Coloured Circle** — Hide/show expressions or access styling options
